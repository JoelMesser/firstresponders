Clogged gutters are the cheapest big problem to prevent. When water can't move
through the gutter run and out the downspout, it goes where you least want it:
behind the fascia, down the siding, and against the foundation.

## The rule of thumb: twice a year

For most Central Maryland homes, plan on:

- **Late fall** — after the leaves are down, before the first hard freeze. This
  is the cleaning that matters most.
- **Spring** — to clear seed pods, blossoms, and shingle grit before summer
  thunderstorm season.

## When twice isn't enough

Bump to three or four cleanings a year if any of these apply:

- **Heavy tree cover**, especially oaks and maples overhanging the roof line.
- **Pines nearby** — needles slip past most gutter guards and mat into a layer
  that water can't penetrate.
- **A low-pitch or complicated roof** that sheds debris into valleys and
  dead-ends.
- **A rental property** — tenants almost never report a clogged gutter. It
  shows up later as a stained ceiling or a wet basement. Put gutters on the
  turnover checklist and on a seasonal schedule.

## What a real gutter cleaning includes

Scooping leaves out of the run is half the job. A proper visit should also:

1. **Flush every downspout** and verify water actually exits at the bottom.
2. **Check pitch and hangers** — a sagging run holds water even when it's clean.
3. **Look at the discharge** — water should land well away from the foundation,
   not pool against it.
4. **Document with photos** so you can see what was done (especially useful for
   landlords who weren't on site).

## The cost of skipping it

Deferred gutter maintenance shows up as fascia rot, basement moisture, eroded
landscaping, and ice dams in winter — repairs measured in thousands, to avoid a
service visit measured in hundreds.
