Every day a rental sits empty costs you money. But rushing a turnover and
skipping the walkthrough costs more — small problems inherited by the next
tenant become your emergency calls later. Here's the checklist we work from.

## 1. Document first

Before anything gets fixed or cleaned, walk the property and photograph
everything: walls, floors, appliances, fixtures, the yard, the fence. This
protects the security deposit conversation and gives you a true punch list.

## 2. The inside punch list

- Test every door, lock, and window — including latches and screens.
- Patch and touch up walls; full repaint every few tenancies.
- Check faucets, drains, and the water heater for leaks or slow drips.
- Test smoke and CO detectors and replace batteries (Maryland requires
  10-year sealed units in many cases — check your county).
- Service or at least filter-swap the HVAC.
- Deep clean: kitchen, baths, flooring. "Broom clean" is not rent-ready.

## 3. The outside punch list

Outside is what your next tenant sees first — and what code enforcement sees
too:

- Mow, edge, and clear overgrowth from beds, walkways, and the fence line.
- Pull volunteer saplings before they're trees against the foundation.
- Power wash the walk, porch, and any green-stained siding.
- Clean and flush the gutters; confirm downspouts drain away from the house.
- Walk the fence: posts solid, gates latching, no leaning sections.
- Haul away anything the last tenant left behind.

## 4. Inspect the big stuff annually

A turnover is the cheapest moment to catch expensive problems, because the
property is empty. Once a year, add a roof and drainage inspection to the
turnover scope — lifted shingles, soft fascia, and ponding water are far
cheaper to fix now than after they show up on a tenant's ceiling.

## Compressing the vacancy window

The biggest time sink in a turnover isn't the work — it's coordinating four
different vendors who each want their own week. One crew that can clean,
repair, haul, and handle the yard in a single scheduled visit turns a
two-week vacancy into a few days.
